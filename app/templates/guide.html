The guide

How Ride Leader Kit works

Ride Leader Kit helps a club ride leader get a ride ready and put the whole group on the same page. You bring a route, it checks what it can, you decide, and everyone gets the same plan on their phone.

A ride in four steps

  1. Bring a routeDrop in a GPX file from Strava, Garmin, Komoot or anywhere else, or pull a route straight from your Strava. To change the line, press Edit route: drag it, add a point to pass through, and it follows the roads. Press Add cue on the map to put in your own warnings and notes, such as “sharp left, gravel”.
  2. Find stopsOn the Stops tab, Find stops along route looks in OpenStreetMap beside your route for cafés, water, defibrillators, bike shops and historic sites, and adds your club’s own saved stops. Tick the ones you want on the ride sheet and mark the one the group will actually stop at.
  3. Check itThe Ready to ride? card on the Checks tab lists what needs a look and what is fine: warnings, forecast, daylight, closures, café hours and defibrillators. Pick the day and start time on the same tab and the checks update.
  4. Share itOn the Share tab, make a link, copy the WhatsApp message, print the cue card or add the ride to a calendar. Add a word from the leader first: “mudguards on, nobody gets left behind”.

It prompts, you decide. Ride Leader Kit helps you do the checks leaders are taught. It doesn’t replace a recce or your own risk assessment, and it never calls a ride “safe”.

What the checks mean

Each check shows OK, Check or Unknown. Unknown is never shown as fine: if a source can’t be reached or has nothing to say, you are told.

Pace groups and arrival times

Each group has an average moving speed and a stop time. The time a group reaches any point is the distance divided by its speed, plus the stop time for every planned stop it has already passed. “Back about” is the same sum for the finish.

The starting groups are A at 28 km/h, B at 24 km/h and C at 20 km/h, each stopping 20 minutes. Change them in Ride plan. The estimate does not adjust for hills or wind, so use the speed that group really averages on a ride like this one.

Sharing

The ride sheet link
One page for the whole group. Anyone who has the link can open it, with no sign-in and nothing to install, and you can pass it on freely. It shows the plan, warnings and weather as they are when it is opened, and keeps the last copy for when there is no signal. You can switch the link off at any time and it stops working for everyone.
WhatsApp message
One tap copies a short summary and the link, ready to paste into the group.
Cue card
A printable A5 card with the key times, stops and your note. Also on the ride sheet.
Add to calendar
The ride sheet has an Add to calendar link, so riders get the day, time and meeting point in their own calendar.
Your club
Your routes are shown to the other members of your club unless you switch Show this route to members of your club off on the Share tab. Nobody outside your club sees them.

Clubs and invite codes

Ride Leader Kit is run club by club. Everyone belongs to at least one club, and a club shares its routes, trusted stops, closures and settings among its own members only.

Joining
Ask a club admin for an invite code. Enter it with your email the first time you sign in, and we email you a link. After that you only need your email. There are no passwords.
Members
Plan rides, save stops, enter closures and share with the club. Add your name on the Account page so the club sees who is leading each ride instead of an email address.
Club admins
Everything members do, plus making invite codes (shown once, with an expiry and optional limits), making other members admins and removing people. Someone who is removed from every club is signed out on their next click.
Site admin
Sets up new clubs and can manage any of them.

Strava

Connect Strava to pull in your own routes. They are kept for up to seven days and only you can see them. When you share one, the site makes your own copy, with your stops, cues and notes, as a route file you own, and shares that. It is separate from Strava and stays until you delete it. Disconnecting Strava, or revoking access on Strava, deletes what we hold from it. The app works fully without Strava.

Where the information comes from, and its limits

  • Maps, stops, roadworks and café hours: OpenStreetMap, which volunteers keep up to date. Sometimes it is wrong or out of date.
  • Weather warnings: Met Éireann, shown as issued (Ireland only).
  • Forecast and elevation: Open-Meteo.
  • Routing when you edit a route: BRouter, on OpenStreetMap roads.
  • Your club’s own stops and closures: only as good as the last time someone checked them. Each saved stop shows when it was last verified.

In an emergency call 112 or 999. Nothing on this site replaces that.

Glossary

Planned stop
A place the group actually stops, usually the café. It adds the stop time to every later arrival time.
Regroup point
A suggested place to wait for the slower group, usually the top of a climb.
Cue
A note on the map, such as a hazard, a turn, water or first aid, that goes on the ride sheet and your device.
Via point
A spot you drag the route through when editing, so it follows the road you want.
Pace group
A group riding at a similar speed, shown with its speed in a red ring.
