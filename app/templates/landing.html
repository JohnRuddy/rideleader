“Is it still on? Where do we stop? Is the café even open?”

That is a lot to hold in your head and a WhatsApp thread. Ride Leader Kit answers it from the route itself, and tells you plainly when it can’t.

What it checks along your route

The things a good leader checks, done before you leave the house.

  • Weather warningsMet Éireann warnings for the counties you ride through, shown as issued, plus the forecast hour by hour at each point on the route.
  • DaylightSunset against your pace group’s finish time, so a late start in October doesn’t become a ride home in the dark.
  • Closures and roadworksRoads marked closed or private along the line, counted only where the route really runs along them.
  • Café hours against your ETAOpen, closed or unknown at the time each group arrives. About one café in four has hours on the map; the rest say Unknown. We never guess.
  • Defibrillators and servicesThe nearest defibrillator in every 10 km, plus cafés, water and bike shops, ready to tick for the ride sheet.
  • Climbs and regroupsEvery real climb with its gradient, and suggested regroup points at the top, so the slower group is never chasing.

It prompts. You decide. Ride Leader Kit helps you do the checks leaders are taught. It doesn’t replace a recce or your own risk assessment, and it never says a ride is “safe”.

From route to ride sheet

Three steps, about as long as a coffee.

  1. 1Bring a route

    Drop in a GPX file, or pull a route straight from Strava. Fix it on the map if you want to, and add your own cues with a right-click.

  2. 2Check it

    The Ready to ride card lists what needs a look and what is fine. Anything it can’t know is marked unknown, never green.

  3. 3Share it

    Send the link, paste the WhatsApp text, print the cue card, or send the finished route back to your head unit.

Private by default

Your routes are yours.

Your club, and only your club

Routes, stops and closures are shared inside your club, and each club is separate from the others. A route is private with one switch, and the ride sheet link is yours to hand out or switch off. A Strava route is never shown as it is: sharing makes your own copy.

Delete it all in one click

Remove your account and every route and ride with it, from the account page. We email you a confirmation.

No ads, no tracking of riders

The ride sheet loads no scripts at all. The map data is OpenStreetMap, the warnings are Met Éireann’s.

Questions leaders ask

Straight answers.

How do I get in?

Ride Leader Kit is run club by club. Ask your club admin for an invite code, enter it with your email the first time you sign in, and we email you a link. After that you only need your email. If your club isn’t set up yet, get in touch with the site owner.

Who sees my routes, stops and closures?

The members of your club, and nobody else. Routes you plan are shared with your club unless you switch them to private, and your club’s trusted stops and road closures are shared the same way. The ride sheet link is separate: anyone you give it to can open it, and you can switch it off any time.

Does it replace my risk assessment or the recce?

No. It makes the checks quicker and harder to forget, and the leader still decides. It will not tell you a ride is safe.

Does it work outside Ireland?

The routing, stops, daylight and forecast work anywhere OpenStreetMap does. The official weather warnings come from Met Éireann, so they cover Ireland only.

Does my club need to switch from Cyql or WhatsApp?

No. Plan here, then post the plan wherever your club already talks.

What if the data is wrong?

Map data is crowd-sourced, so sometimes it is. A closure can be stale and a café may have changed its hours. That is why unknowns are marked and why the route is checked against the map you can see.

What does it cost?

It is free while we pilot it with clubs.
